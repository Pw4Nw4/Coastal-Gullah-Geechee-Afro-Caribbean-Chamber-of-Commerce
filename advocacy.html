A collective voice for our businesses.

We advocate for Gullah Geechee, Afro-Caribbean, Black, Brown, minority-owned and women-owned businesses throughout the Georgia Coast and beyond.

What we speak up for

We're not here to be against anything. We're here to be for something, and to bring people together.

Drafted from the chamber's "Collective Economic Voice" pillar, its membership benefits and its leaders' words. Confirm the priorities with chamber leadership.

  • Being seen

    Representation matters. We work so Gullah Geechee and Afro-Caribbean business owners are seen, counted and present wherever decisions about business are made.

  • Credit for the culture

    Shrimp and grits, red rice and sweetgrass baskets are Gullah Geechee, and so is the credit. We stand up for the people who made the culture.

  • Fair access to contracts

    Certifications and procurement open doors. We advocate for fair access to contracts for minority- and women-owned businesses, and help members get certified.

  • Ownership and land

    Heirs' property and ancestral land are at the heart of the legacy. We support education and partners that help families keep what they own.

  • Funding and capital

    Grants, microloans and community lenders shouldn't depend on who you know. We share funding leads and speak up for fair access to capital.

  • Visibility for heritage business

    Artisans, hospitality professionals and heritage-centered businesses deserve visitors and customers. Our cultural tourism work puts them in front of both.

Stronger together

One business speaking up is easy to miss. Many speaking together are heard.

Members bring us what they're facing: a permit, a contract, a rule, a barrier. We bring those concerns together, so the people who make decisions hear one clear message from many businesses.

A collective voice on the policies that affect coastal businesses is part of every membership.
